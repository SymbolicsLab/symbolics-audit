## The one open question — everything formal waits on it

**Semiorder or total order?**

PB's grounding gives comparability **in principle**, cross-seam included. A **total order** needs one
thing more that is not there: **that discrimination be transitive**. Resolution is an index parameter
with finite grain, and indistinguishability at a threshold is non-transitive — which is exactly what
the small-improvement arguments report. So the operative object is a **semiorder**: comparable in
principle, with a discrimination threshold.

**Claude recommends the semiorder.** It keeps PB's metaphysical position intact, accounts for the
parity data as sub-threshold discrimination rather than incommensurability, and — the part that keeps
it honest — carries a **falsifiable rider**: the threshold should track index resolution, so a finer
index prices differences a coarser one reports as ties (the expert discriminates stakes the novice
cannot). ⚠ **Without the rider the move is absorption and sits on the wrong side of the quarantine.
Never state it without the rider.**

The total order is cleaner and says the ties are real ties — making small-improvement cases have a
fact of the matter nobody can access, and reducing the coarseness residue to pure epistemics.
